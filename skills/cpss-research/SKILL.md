# Research

Research broadly enough to answer the user's actual question, but stop when additional work is unlikely to materially change the answer, major contradictions have been surfaced, and important uncertainties are known.

## Workflow

1. Define the research question and preserve the user's objective, constraints, decision context, and stated assumptions.
2. Ask only when a missing fact would materially change the research direction. Otherwise proceed with a bounded interpretation.
3. Use current external research when the subject is time-sensitive, niche, disputed, recommendation-sensitive, or otherwise not reliably answerable from stable knowledge alone.
4. Prefer primary, official, original, or directly authoritative sources for material factual claims. Use strong secondary sources for context, comparison, interpretation, discovery, or independent corroboration.
5. Separate verified facts, user-provided facts, assumptions, inferences, uncertainties, and recommendations when confusing them would change the conclusion.
6. Record scope details such as timeframe, geography, population, version, definitions, units, and comparison baseline when material.
7. Surface credible contradictory evidence and important source limitations rather than smoothing them away.
8. Synthesize the bottom line, key findings, tradeoffs, and material uncertainty.
9. Stop at practical sufficiency: when additional searching is unlikely to change the answer and remaining gaps are explicit.

## Research record

For substantive research, preserve a complete downstream record containing:

- research question and scope;
- bottom line;
- material findings with source provenance and relevant dates;
- contradictory or limiting evidence;
- assumptions and inferences;
- unresolved questions;
- source record;
- a note on what a later validator should test most carefully.

When file creation is available, save a self-contained Markdown checkpoint. Each later checkpoint should contain the complete current state rather than only a diff.

## Output

Default to a concise answer with the bottom line, most important findings, material uncertainty/disagreement, and source provenance. Provide deeper detail when requested or when the consequence of the decision justifies it.

## Boundaries

- Do not fabricate sources, access, findings, dates, quotes, measurements, or current status.
- Do not hide evidence gaps to make the answer smoother.
- Do not force exhaustive research when the answer is already decision-sufficient.
- Do not perform a formal validation audit unless requested or required by the active workflow.
