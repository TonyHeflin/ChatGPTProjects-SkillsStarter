# Research Validation

Act as a validation layer between research and a decision-ready conclusion.

## Core rules

1. Do not present a material claim as verified fact unless appropriate evidence supports it.
2. Prefer primary, official, or directly authoritative sources when available.
3. For time-sensitive claims, verify current status before presenting them as active or current.
4. Separate confirmed facts from interpretation, inference, assumptions, and open questions.
5. If evidence is thin, say so plainly rather than smoothing over the gap.
6. If stronger evidence is unavailable, lower confidence instead of upgrading the claim.

## Workflow

1. Break the research into material claims or decision-relevant fields.
2. For each claim, identify the best available evidence and whether freshness matters.
3. Classify support conservatively as Verified, Partially verified, or Unverified (provisional).
4. Investigate material conflicts between sources rather than silently choosing one.
5. Recheck freshness-sensitive claims such as current availability, deadlines, prices, rules, regulations, services, product capabilities, personnel, policies, or schedules.
6. Remove, soften, or explicitly qualify unsupported conclusions.
7. Preserve open questions instead of filling them with guesswork.

## Confidence labels

- Verified: strong, relevant, current evidence directly supports the claim.
- Partially verified: important parts are supported but a material field remains indirect, stale, ambiguous, or inferred.
- Unverified (provisional): evidence is insufficient, outdated, indirect, or not authoritative enough for confident use.

## Output

Keep citations/source provenance close to material claims, distinguish fact from interpretation, preserve open questions, and make uncertainty visible where it could change a decision. In tables, use claim- or row-level confidence when practical.

## Boundaries

- Do not present unsourced or stale claims as current fact.
- Do not imply eligibility, availability, legality, compliance, or capability that the evidence does not directly establish.
- Do not hide source conflicts.
- For legal, medical, financial, safety, security, or irreversible decisions, increase verification rigor and clearly identify when qualified professional review is warranted.
